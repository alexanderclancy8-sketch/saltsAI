# Emergency lighting: BS 5266-1, BS EN 1838 and BS EN 50172

Summary: A UK guide to emergency escape lighting design basics, routine testing (monthly flick test, annual full-duration test), 3 h vs 1 h duration, maintained and non-maintained luminaires, self-test systems, certificates and the logbook.

Status: Written September 2026 from general industry knowledge. Figures marked **(verify)** need checking against the current edition. Revised editions of BS EN 1838 and BS EN 50172 were published in 2024, and BS 5266-1 may be revised to match, so confirm which editions apply to new designs.

## Quick facts (most asked)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Main UK code of practice | BS 5266-1:2016 (check for a newer edition) **(verify)** |
| Lighting levels and signs standard | BS EN 1838 (2013 edition widely used; a 2024 revision exists) **(verify)** |
| System operation and testing standard | BS EN 50172 (2004 edition, also published as BS 5266-8; a 2024 revision exists) **(verify)** |
| Monthly test | Short functional ("flick") test of **every** luminaire and illuminated sign by simulating mains failure. Record the result |
| Annual test | **Full rated duration** test (for example 3 hours). Every luminaire must still be lit at the end. Record the result |
| Normal rated duration | **3 hours** for most premises |
| When 1 hour may be acceptable | Non-sleeping premises where evacuation is immediate and the building won't be re-occupied until batteries have recharged, if the risk assessment allows |
| Recharge time | Self-contained luminaires are designed to recharge within about 24 hours **(verify for the product)** |
| Escape route level | At least 1 lux on the centre line of routes up to 2 m wide, with the central band at least half the route width at 0.5 lux or more |
| Open (anti-panic) areas | At least 0.5 lux on the floor of the core area (excluding a 0.5 m border). BS 5266-1 applies this to open areas larger than 60 m² and to smaller areas with particular hazards **(verify)** |
| High-risk task areas | 10% of normal maintained illuminance or 15 lux, whichever is greater. Must reach level within 0.5 s |
| Response time | 50% of required level within 5 s, full level within 60 s (escape routes and open areas) |
| Uniformity | Max to min ratio of 40:1 or less on escape routes and open areas |
| Safety equipment | At least 5 lux on the floor at fire-fighting equipment, fire alarm call points and first-aid posts |
| Sign viewing distance | Internally illuminated signs: 200 × sign height. Externally illuminated: 100 × sign height |

*Illuminance figures are from BS EN 1838:2013 as commonly applied in the UK. **Verify whether the 2024 revision changes any value before using it for a new design.***

## Why emergency lighting is required

- **Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005:** emergency routes and exits that need illumination must have emergency lighting of adequate intensity if normal lighting fails. The responsible person must keep it maintained in efficient working order.
- **Building Regulations Approved Document B:** sets out where escape lighting is needed in new and altered buildings.
- **Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992:** require emergency lighting where people would be especially exposed to danger if artificial lighting failed.
- **Health and Safety (Safety Signs and Signals) Regulations 1996:** escape signs, with running-man pictograms to BS EN ISO 7010 and escape route signing to BS 5499-4.

## Types of emergency lighting

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Emergency escape lighting | Lets people leave safely. Covers escape routes, open (anti-panic) areas and high-risk task areas |
| Standby lighting | Lets normal activities continue during a mains failure. Not escape lighting unless it also meets the escape standards |
| Self-contained luminaire | Has its own battery, charger and control gear. The most common type in small and medium premises |
| Central battery system | Luminaires fed from a central battery via fire-resisting wiring. Common in large buildings. Needs daily indicator checks and battery room care |

**Operating modes:**

| Mode | Behaviour | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Maintained (M)** | Lamp lit at all times the premises are in use, on mains and in emergency | Theatres, cinemas, places of entertainment, exit signs where normal lighting may be dimmed |
| **Non-maintained (NM)** | Lit only when the normal lighting supply fails | Offices, factories, most commercial premises |
| **Sustained / combined** | Two or more lamps, at least one energised only in emergency | Less common now with LED |
| **Switched maintained** | Wired with switched and permanent live so it acts as normal lighting (switched) and emergency lighting (permanent live monitors supply) | Corridors and stairs where fittings double as normal lighting |

- Shorthand on drawings: **M3** means maintained, 3 hours. **NM3** means non-maintained, 3 hours. **NM1** means non-maintained, 1 hour.
- Luminaires to BS EN 60598-2-22 carry a coded rating label. It shows self-contained or central supply, the mode, facilities (for example a test device or remote rest mode) and duration in minutes. Read it off the label or data sheet rather than guessing **(verify the code format)**.

## Where emergency lighting is needed (points of emphasis)

Luminaires should be placed so the following are illuminated:
- each exit door intended for emergency use, and outside each final exit to a place of safety
- near stairs, so each flight gets direct light (each tread lit)
- near any other change of floor level
- at each change of direction and each intersection of corridors
- escape route signs and exit signs
- near fire-fighting equipment, fire alarm call points and first-aid points (5 lux at floor level)
- lift cars (where people may be carried), escalators and moving walkways
- toilets larger than 8 m², and all accessible (disabled) toilets and similar facilities regardless of size **(verify)**
- plant rooms, switch rooms, generator rooms and control rooms
- covered car parks (pedestrian routes)
- open areas larger than 60 m² (anti-panic), and high-risk task areas such as dangerous machinery, laboratories and kitchens with hazards

## Design basics

- Design is done by a competent person using the fire risk assessment. The output is drawings showing luminaire and sign positions, classification, duration and photometric justification (spacing tables or lighting calculations).
- **Self-contained luminaires** should be fed from the unswitched live of the **local** final lighting circuit, so a local circuit failure (for example an MCB trip) brings the emergency lights on in that area.
- **Central battery wiring** should be fire-resisting, in the same way as fire alarm cabling. Choose standard or enhanced grade by risk.
- Provide **test key switches** (fishtail or key-operated) so the permanent live can be interrupted for testing without switching off the normal lighting. They should be labelled and secure.
- Plan for testing: where annual full-duration tests would leave an area without cover during recharge, design to allow alternate-luminaire testing, or schedule out of hours.
- Consider an automatic test system (BS EN 62034) for sites with many luminaires or difficult access.

## Routine testing and inspection

| Frequency | Who (typical) | What |
|---|---|---|
| Daily (central battery systems) | Site staff | Visual check that system indicators show normal (charging, no fault) |
| **Monthly** | Site staff or contractor | Simulate mains failure (test key or breaker) for long enough to confirm each luminaire and sign lights. Keep it short (no more than a quarter of rated duration). Restore supply, check charge indicators, record results **(verify duration limit)** |
| **Annually** | Competent contractor | Full rated duration test (3 h or 1 h). Check every luminaire is still lit at the end. Restore supply, check charging, record failures, issue a periodic inspection and test certificate |
| On change | Designer or contractor | Re-assess coverage after alterations, changes of layout or use |

**Practical tips:**
- Do annual tests at low-risk times, as the building has reduced emergency cover for up to about 24 hours during recharge. Alternatively test alternate luminaires in each area on different days.
- Walk the building in a planned route with a marked-up drawing, so no fitting is missed. Number luminaires on the drawing and on the fitting.
- Record each failure by location and luminaire ID. Raise a remedial quote with the type, battery or lamp and quantity.
- Common failures:
  - batteries not lasting the full duration (NiCd cells commonly need replacing around every 4 years; lithium iron phosphate usually lasts longer — follow the manufacturer)
  - failed LED driver or charger
  - no permanent live (fitting never charging)
  - damaged diffuser
  - missing or wrong exit sign legend (old "EXIT" or text-only signs should be replaced with ISO 7010 pictogram signs)
- The older practice of 6-monthly one-hour tests in the first years is **not** in the current BS EN 50172 regime **(verify)**. Don't sell it as a requirement.

## Self-test and automatic test systems

- Self-test luminaires (BS EN 62034) run the monthly functional and annual duration tests automatically, usually staggered to avoid all luminaires being discharged at once, and show status on an LED indicator.
- **Someone still has to check and record the results.** The monthly "test" becomes a walk-round (or dashboard check) of status indicators, and faults still need a competent person to fix them.
- Addressable or centrally monitored systems (wired, DALI-based or wireless) produce reports and fault logs. Keep printouts or exports in the logbook.
- Indicator codes (flash patterns and colours) vary by manufacturer. Keep the manufacturer's legend in the logbook.

## Certificates and logbook

- BS 5266-1 includes model certificates. Typical documents:
  - **Completion certificate** for a new or altered installation. This may be a combined certificate covering design, installation and commissioning, or separate ones where different parties did each stage.
  - **Declaration of design**, **installation** and **verification** of installation, where issued separately.
  - **Periodic inspection and test certificate**, issued after the annual test (and optionally after monthly tests).
  - A **modification** or **extension** record when changes are made.
- The **logbook** is kept on site by the responsible person. It should record:
  - the date of each monthly and annual test and the result
  - faults found and remedial action taken, with dates
  - alterations to the installation
  - the name of the person or company that tested
- Hand over as-fitted drawings showing luminaire positions, type and duration, plus the O&M information (replacement battery types, test key locations).
- A BAFE scheme exists for emergency lighting companies (SP203-4) **(verify scheme reference and scope)**. ICEL (the Industry Committee for Emergency Lighting) publishes industry guidance.

## Typical service visit (annual) checklist

1. Review the logbook: have monthly tests been done and recorded? Are faults outstanding?
2. Check the design still matches the building (new rooms, changed layouts, blocked signs).
3. Check the charging indicator on every luminaire (green LED or similar).
4. Run the full-duration test. Note any luminaire that fails before the end.
5. Check signs are correct, visible and legible, with the right arrow direction.
6. Restore mains and confirm all luminaires are charging.
7. For a central battery: check battery condition, charger output, cell voltages or impedance per the manufacturer, and ventilation.
8. Issue the certificate, update the logbook and list defects with priority. Missing cover on an escape route is urgent.

## Fault triage

| Symptom | Likely cause | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Charge LED off | No permanent live, blown fuse, failed charger | Measure supply at the fitting. Check the local circuit and test key switch position |
| Fails duration test early | Battery at end of life, or recently fitted battery not yet cycled | Check battery date. Replace with a matched type. Retest after a full charge |
| Lamp doesn't light on test | LED module or driver failure, battery disconnected (transit link) | Check the battery plug is connected. Replace the module or fitting |
| Self-test LED shows fault | Battery, lamp or charge fault (per the manufacturer's code) | Look up the manufacturer's code. Reset after repair |
| Whole area fails | Test key left in test position, circuit off, central battery fault | Check key switches and the distribution board. Check the central system panel |

## Common questions

- **Can the customer's own staff do the monthly test?**
  - Yes, if they are instructed on how to do it safely and record it.
  - The annual full-duration test and repairs should be done by a competent person.
  - Many maintenance contracts offer monthly testing as an add-on.
- **Is a 1-hour system acceptable?**
  - Only where the fire risk assessment supports it.
  - It is not acceptable for sleeping premises or places of entertainment, or wherever the premises might be re-occupied straight after a mains failure.
  - When in doubt, specify 3 hours.
- **Do LED conversions or new fittings change anything?**
  - Replacement fittings must still meet the design (light output, spacing, duration and mode).
  - Swapping a fitting for one with a lower emergency output can leave gaps in cover.
  - Check the manufacturer's spacing tables.
- **Does emergency lighting need a separate certificate from the fire alarm?**
  - Yes. They are separate systems with separate standards and certificates, even when the same visit covers both.
- **Is a fire risk assessment action "emergency lighting inadequate" a job for us?**
  - Yes. Survey the premises, then quote a design to BS 5266-1 with luminaire positions and types.
  - Supply completion certificates and an updated logbook at handover.
