# Fire extinguishers and other fire protection systems: BS 5306, BS EN 3, door hold-opens, aspirating, voice alarm, EVC and suppression interfaces

Summary: A UK guide to portable fire extinguisher servicing (BS 5306-3), selection and siting (BS 5306-8), BS EN 3, and short practical notes on fire doors and hold-open devices, aspirating smoke detection, voice alarm (BS 5839-8), refuge/emergency voice communication (BS 5839-9) and fire suppression interfaces.

Status: Written September 2026 from general knowledge. BS 5306-8 and BS 5839-8 have had recent revisions, so confirm which edition applies. Anything marked **(verify)** should be checked against the current edition.

## Quick facts: extinguishers (most asked)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Servicing standard | BS 5306-3 (2017 edition) **(verify)** |
| Selection and siting standard | BS 5306-8 (2012 edition, with a later revision published **(verify which applies)**) |
| Product standard | BS EN 3 (portable extinguishers). Colour coding to BS 7863 |
| Basic service | **Annually**, at intervals not exceeding 12 months, by a competent person |
| Extended service (discharge test and refill) | **Every 5 years** for water, foam, wet chemical and stored-pressure powder extinguishers **(verify special rules for primary-sealed powder units)** |
| CO2 extinguishers | **Overhaul every 10 years**, including a hydraulic pressure test of the cylinder. No 5-year discharge |
| Replacement age | The 2017 edition sets out an end-of-life / maximum service life. Many firms replace at 20 years from manufacture **(verify)** |
| User check | **Monthly** visual check by the responsible person, and recorded |
| New extinguishers | Must be **commissioned on site** by a competent person, not just delivered in a box |
| Travel distance (Class A) | No more than 30 m to reach an extinguisher |
| Mounting height | Carrying handle about 1.0 m above floor for heavier units, about 1.5 m for lighter units **(verify weight split)** |
| Minimum Class A provision | Rule of thumb: total Class A rating per storey at least 0.065 × floor area (m²), and normally at least two Class A extinguishers per storey **(verify)** |

## Fire classes and extinguisher types

| Class | Fire involving | Suitable extinguishers |
|---|---|---|
| A | Solids: wood, paper, textiles | Water, foam, water mist, (powder) |
| B | Flammable liquids | Foam, CO2, powder, water mist (check rating) |
| C | Flammable gases | Powder, but only to aid escape once the gas supply is isolated |
| D | Metals | Specialist powders (e.g. L2, M28) |
| F | Cooking oils and fats | Wet chemical |
| Electrical | Live electrical equipment (not a formal class) | CO2, powder, some dielectrically tested water/foam/mist units |

| Type | Colour band (BS 7863) |
|---|---|
| Water | Red (all red) |
| Foam | Cream |
| Powder | Blue |
| CO2 | Black |
| Wet chemical | Canary yellow |
| Water mist | Commonly white **(no single mandated colour — verify)** |

**Selection notes:**
- The body of an extinguisher to BS EN 3 is red. A coloured band or zone identifies the agent.
- Avoid **powder in enclosed spaces** such as offices and small rooms, because of visibility and inhalation. BS 5306-8 advises against it. Prefer foam, water mist or CO2 where appropriate.
- Lithium-ion battery fires: there is no BS EN 3 class. Some agents are marketed for them (for example aqueous vermiculite). Follow the risk assessment and manufacturer evidence, and don't overclaim.
- Fire blankets: BS EN 1869. They are commonly supplied for small kitchens. Replace at the manufacturer's end-of-life and check them at service.

## BS 5306-3: servicing regime

**Commissioning (new extinguishers):**
- check the extinguisher is undamaged and correctly charged
- fit the tamper seal
- mount it in the right position and site it to BS 5306-8
- attach a service label and record it on the site register

**Basic service (annual):**
- check the location, access and mounting, and that the extinguisher is suitable for the risk
- inspect externally for damage, corrosion, legible instructions, and the hose, horn and nozzle
- weigh it, or check the pressure gauge or indicator. Check any gas cartridge (cartridge units) by weighing
- check the safety pin and tamper seal. Replace the seal
- check the manufacture date, and whether the extended service or overhaul is due
- update the service label and give the customer a report listing condemned or missing units and recommendations

**Extended service (usually 5 years for water-based and powder):**
- discharge test, internal inspection, refill or recharge, replace seals and O-rings
- it is often more economical to replace small extinguishers than to discharge-test them. Offer the customer the choice and record it

**Overhaul (CO2 at 10 years):**
- hydraulic pressure test of the cylinder, refill, new valve seals where needed
- many firms replace instead, depending on cost

**Condemn and replace when:**
- the extinguisher is damaged, corroded or obsolete
- it is beyond its service life, or fails its tests
- it is a non-standard type
- it is the wrong agent for the risk (for example old halon, which is illegal to keep except critical-use exemptions)

**Competence:**
- technicians should be trained and assessed. The BAFE SP101 scheme and its associated technician standard (ST104) are the recognised UK route **(verify reference)**.
- The FETA industry body publishes guidance.

## BS 5306-8: selection and siting principles

- Site extinguishers on escape routes, near exits and in the same relative place on each floor, so people pass them when escaping.
- Put Class B, F or special-risk extinguishers close to the hazard. For example, a wet chemical unit near a deep fat fryer, and CO2 near electrical plant or server racks.
- Mount them on brackets or stands. Don't leave them on the floor where they will be moved or used as doorstops.
- Mark positions with a sign (BS EN ISO 7010 fire equipment sign) where they are not obvious.
- Don't over-provide the wrong type. Two suitable extinguishers are better than four unsuitable ones.

## Fire doors and hold-open devices

**Fire doors:**
- They are tested and certified as complete door sets or assemblies: FD30, FD60, with "S" for smoke seals (for example FD30S).
- Check them for:
  - self-closing to fully latch from any angle
  - gaps, typically about 3 mm (roughly 2–4 mm) along the sides and head **(verify for the specific door certification)**
  - intact intumescent strips and smoke seals
  - no damage, holes or non-certificated hardware
  - correct signage ("Fire door keep shut")
  - correct hinges (at least three)
  - glazing certified for fire rating
- Relevant standards: BS 8214 (fire door installation and maintenance), BS EN 1154 (door closers), BS EN 1155 (electrically powered hold-open devices), BS EN 1634 (fire resistance testing).
- In England, the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 require, in multi-occupied residential buildings over 11 m, quarterly checks of communal fire doors and annual "best endeavours" checks of flat entrance doors.

**Hold-open devices and free-swing closers:**
- Hold-open devices let fire doors stay open in normal use but close automatically on a fire alarm. The options are:
  - **Electromagnetic door holders** (wired to the fire alarm system). They release on fire alarm, power failure and a manual release button.
  - **Free-swing door closers.** The door moves freely like an unclosed door, but the closer engages on a fire signal or power loss. Useful for accessibility, such as care homes.
  - **Acoustic (battery, self-contained) hold-open devices.** They listen for the fire alarm sounder and release. They are cheap to retrofit, but depend on batteries and correct sound recognition. Check them weekly with the fire alarm test and replace batteries per the manufacturer. Many also release at a set time each night.
- Interface design follows BS 7273-4. The category of actuation depends on how critical the door is to life safety (see `standards/cctv-and-access-control.md`). Hold-open devices **must fail safe**, meaning the door closes on loss of power or signal.
- Test releases at each weekly fire alarm test (user) and at every service visit (engineer). Record doors that fail to close fully and latch. That is a fire safety defect, not just a door fault.

## Aspirating smoke detection (ASD)

- **Product standard:** BS EN 54-20, with classes:
  - **A**: very high sensitivity, for example clean rooms and high airflow
  - **B**: enhanced sensitivity
  - **C**: normal sensitivity, equivalent to point detectors
- **Typical uses:** server rooms and data halls, warehouses with high ceilings, heritage buildings (discreet sampling points), prisons and custodial cells (tamper-resistant), cold stores, atria, and lift shafts.
- **Design:** pipework with sampling holes, calculated with the manufacturer's software. BS 5839-1 limits the **transport time from the most remote sampling hole** (commonly quoted as a maximum of 120 s) **(verify)**.
- **Maintenance:**
  - service with the fire alarm, at intervals not exceeding 6 months
  - check airflow readings against commissioning baselines
  - replace filters
  - test the transport time from the end-of-run sampling hole with smoke
  - check pipe integrity, end caps and hole labels
  - clean or blow through pipes where needed
  - follow the manufacturer and FIA code of practice for ASD
- **Common faults:** airflow high (broken pipe or missing end cap) or airflow low (blocked holes or filter), filter due, and detector communication fault.

## Voice alarm (BS 5839-8)

- Voice alarm (VA) replaces tones with **pre-recorded and live voice messages**. It is used where occupants are unfamiliar with the building or phased/staged evacuation is needed: shopping centres, stadia, transport hubs, large offices and theatres.
- **Standards:** BS 5839-8 (code of practice, with a revised edition published in recent years **(verify the current edition)**), BS EN 54-16 (VA control equipment) and BS EN 54-24 (loudspeakers).
- **Speech intelligibility** is measured as STI (speech transmission index). A target of about **0.50 STI** is commonly required, with limited allowances in some areas **(verify)**.
- The **fire detection system triggers the VA system** via a monitored interface. Messages include "alert" and "evacuate", and there may be a firefighter microphone.
- **Maintenance:** in line with the fire alarm (at least 6-monthly). Test messages, zones, amplifier standby changeover, battery and loudspeaker line monitoring, and the microphone.

## Emergency voice communication (EVC) and disabled refuges (BS 5839-9)

- EVC systems provide two-way communication between remote outstations and a master station at the fire control point:
  - **Type A outstations:** firefighter telephones.
  - **Type B outstations:** refuge call points for people who can't use stairs, often in disabled refuges within protected stairs.
- **Required** by building regulations guidance (AD B / AD M) in many buildings with refuges, in taller buildings with firefighting shafts, with evacuation lifts, and in stadia. Follow the fire strategy.
- **Standard:** BS 5839-9 (2021 edition **(verify)**). Use fire-resisting cabling and monitored lines, and fit a standby battery.
- **Maintenance:** test every outstation, check line monitoring and battery, and check the master station. This is typically done alongside fire alarm servicing, at intervals not exceeding 6 months **(verify)**.
- Don't confuse EVC with **accessible toilet alarms** (pull cord). Those are assistance alarms, not EVC, although often installed by the same contractor.

## Evacuation alert systems (BS 8629)

- These are fire and rescue service–operated systems for blocks of flats that use a "stay put" strategy. Firefighters use them to sound evacuation alerts in some or all flats.
- Approved Document B requires them in new residential buildings with a storey 18 m or more above ground (England, from the 2022 amendments) **(verify dates)**.
- Maintenance is per BS 8629. Access and control are restricted to the FRS.

## Fire suppression interfaces

| System | Standard / approvals | Fire alarm interface points |
|---|---|---|
| Gaseous (clean agent, e.g. HFC-227ea, FK-5-1-12, inert gases IG-541/IG-55) | BS EN 15004 series; actuation per BS 7273-1 **(verify edition)** | Coincidence (double-knock) detection, pre-discharge alarm, "gas released" signal, manual/automatic mode indication, abort or hold switch (where used), plant and damper shutdown, door interlocks |
| Kitchen suppression (wet chemical) | BS EN 17446 (commercial kitchen systems); LPCB LPS 1223 **(verify)** | "Discharged" signal to fire alarm, automatic gas (and often electric) shut-off to cooking appliances, manual release |
| Sprinklers | BS EN 12845 / BS 9251 | Flow switches as fire zones. Valve monitoring (tamper) switches as fault/supervisory signals. Pump running and fault signals |
| Pre-action sprinklers | BS EN 12845 / BS 7273-3 **(verify)** | Detection opens the pre-action valve. Usually coincidence logic |
| Water mist | BS 8489 / BS 8458 | Similar to sprinklers or pre-action, depending on system type |

**Practical rules for fire alarm engineers:**
- **Always isolate the extinguishing release before testing detection** in a gas-protected room. Follow the suppression contractor's isolation procedure, then confirm and record the isolation.
- Agree the cause and effect with the suppression contractor. Test the interface signals (pre-alarm, release, fault, discharged) at each service without discharging.
- **Room integrity:** gaseous systems rely on the enclosure holding the agent. Door fan (integrity) tests are typically annual or after alterations **(verify)**. Tell the client that new cable penetrations must be sealed.
- Maintenance of gaseous and kitchen suppression is usually 6-monthly or annual per the standard, the manufacturer and the insurer **(verify)**.
- **F-gas rules:** people working on fire protection systems containing fluorinated gases must hold the appropriate F-gas qualification **(verify current GB rules)**.
- **PFAS:** the fluorinated agent FK-5-1-12 has been affected by a major producer ending PFAS manufacture, and PFAS restrictions are developing. Check the supply and regulatory position before specifying.
