# UK tax and accounting for a limited company fire and security contractor

Summary: A practical reference for running the finances of a UK limited company in fire and security. Covers VAT (including the construction domestic reverse charge), CIS, PAYE/RTI, employer NIC, Corporation Tax, Companies House, pensions auto-enrolment, capital allowances, vans vs cars, management accounts KPIs and credit control.

> **Important:** Tax rates, thresholds and deadlines change every tax year and at every Budget. Figures below are what was known up to the 2025/26 tax year, plus announcements believed to apply from April 2026. **Always check figures against GOV.UK/HMRC for the current tax year (2026/27 at the time of writing).** Statutory filings (VAT returns, CT600, accounts, CIS and payroll) should be prepared or reviewed by a qualified accountant. This file is general information, not tax advice.

## VAT

**Registration and returns:**
- Register when taxable turnover for the last 12 months exceeds **£90,000**, or if you expect to exceed it in the next 30 days alone.
- **Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT** applies to all VAT-registered businesses. Keep digital records and file through compatible software, with digital links (no manual retyping between systems).
- **Quarterly returns** are usual. Monthly returns can help if you're regularly due repayments, for example if much of your work is under the reverse charge. Returns and payment are due **1 month + 7 days** after the period ends.

**Penalties (verify current rates):**
- Late **submission** uses a points system. For quarterly filers, 4 points triggers a £200 penalty, then £200 per further late return.
- Late **payment** penalties start after 15 days. Rates were increased from April 2025 **(verify current %)**.
- HMRC late payment interest is base rate + 4% from April 2025 **(verify)**.

**Useful rules:**
- **Tax point:** normally the earlier of invoice or payment. For stage payments and applications for payment on construction contracts, the tax point is the earlier of payment received or VAT invoice issued. An application for payment is not a VAT invoice if marked accordingly.
- **Maintenance contracts billed in advance:** VAT is due on the invoice tax point, even though the income is earned over the year (see "Deferred income").
- **Bad debt relief:** reclaim output VAT on debts more than 6 months overdue that have been written off in the accounts.
- **Schemes:**
  - cash accounting (turnover up to £1.35m): VAT is paid when customers pay, which helps cash flow
  - annual accounting
  - flat rate (rarely beneficial for a materials-heavy trade)
  Discuss with the accountant.
- **Zero-rating on new dwellings:** work done in the course of constructing a new dwelling can be zero-rated. That includes fitting items that count as "building materials" under HMRC VAT Notice 708. Check whether the specific item (for example a fire alarm or security system) qualifies before zero-rating **(verify)**.

## Construction Industry Scheme (CIS) and fire and security work

**What CIS covers:**
- CIS applies to payments by contractors to subcontractors for **construction operations** (Finance Act 2004, section 74).
- The definition **includes** installing systems of heating, lighting, power supply, ventilation, water supply **and fire protection** in buildings, plus repairs and alterations.
- It **excludes** the **installation of security systems, including burglar alarms, CCTV and public address systems**. It also excludes manufacture and delivery of components, and professional services such as design and surveying.

**Practical reading for Salts (confirm with the accountant):**

| Work | CIS? |
|---|---|
| Fire alarm, emergency lighting, sprinkler or suppression installation and repairs for a contractor | **Within CIS** (fire protection, lighting, power) |
| Intruder alarm, CCTV and PA installation | **Outside CIS** (security systems exclusion) |
| Access control and door entry | **Grey area.** Largely "security systems", but associated electrical or door works may be construction. Take advice |
| Pure inspection or testing visits (servicing without repair) | Generally not construction operations, but mixed maintenance contracts that include repairs need a consistent, documented view. Take advice |
| Portable fire extinguisher supply and servicing | Generally not construction operations **(verify)** |

**When Salts is the subcontractor** (working for a main contractor or FM company on CIS work):
- Salts must be CIS-registered. The contractor verifies Salts with HMRC and deducts 20% (or 0% with gross payment status) from the **labour** element.
- Show materials separately on invoices so deductions are only made on labour.
- As a limited company, CIS deductions suffered are **offset against Salts' own PAYE/NIC liability** each month via the EPS. Any excess can be reclaimed, usually after the tax year ends.
- **Gross payment status (0%)** needs:
  - a business test (UK bank account, business run through it)
  - a turnover test (net construction turnover above set thresholds)
  - a **compliance test** (a good record on tax filings and payments; VAT compliance was added from April 2024)
  **(verify thresholds)**
  Gross status is a big cash-flow benefit.

**When Salts is the contractor** (paying subcontract engineers or firms for CIS work):
1. Verify each subcontractor with HMRC before first payment.
2. Deduct at the rate HMRC gives.
3. Give **payment and deduction statements** within 14 days of the tax month end.
4. File the **CIS300 by the 19th**.
5. Pay deductions with PAYE by the **22nd**.

- **Late CIS return penalties** start at £100 and escalate at 2, 6 and 12 months.
- **Employment status:** check that "self-employed" subcontract engineers are genuinely self-employed. HMRC's CEST tool helps, and there are IR35-style risks.

## VAT domestic reverse charge (DRC) for construction services

The DRC has applied since 1 March 2021. Under it, the **customer** accounts for the VAT instead of the supplier.

**It applies when ALL of these are true:**
1. The supply is a standard- or reduced-rated service (with any materials) that is **reported under CIS**, i.e. a construction operation.
2. Both supplier and customer are **VAT-registered** in the UK.
3. The customer is **registered for CIS** and makes the payment under CIS.
4. The customer is **not an end user or intermediary supplier**, i.e. the customer is going to sell on those construction services.

**It does not apply to:**
- supplies to **end users** (building owners and occupiers who aren't selling on the service, e.g. a factory owner, school or landlord's own premises) or to intermediary suppliers connected with them. The customer should confirm end-user status **in writing**
- zero-rated supplies
- work outside CIS, such as security system installation
- supplies to non-VAT-registered customers
- employment businesses supplying staff

**The 5% disregard:** if reverse-charge services are only a small part of a mixed supply (5% or less of the value), the supply can be treated normally **(verify guidance)**.

**What this means for Salts:**

| Scenario | VAT treatment |
|---|---|
| Fire alarm installation as a **subcontractor to a VAT- and CIS-registered main contractor** on a project they're selling on | **Reverse charge.** Invoice shows the VAT rate and amount for information, with wording such as "Reverse charge: customer to pay the VAT to HMRC". Salts doesn't collect the VAT |
| Fire alarm installation or maintenance **directly for the building owner or occupier** (end user) | Normal VAT at 20% |
| Intruder alarm / CCTV installation for a main contractor | Normally **normal VAT** (outside CIS, so outside DRC), but mixed contracts need care |
| Work for an FM company that is CIS-registered and reselling the service to its client | Check whether the work is a CIS construction operation. If yes, DRC is likely to apply |

**Other points:**
- **Cash-flow effect:** under DRC Salts no longer holds the VAT between invoicing and paying it over. If a large share of sales are reverse-charged, Salts may become a **repayment trader**, so consider monthly VAT returns.
- **Systems:** sales invoices need a reverse-charge VAT code and wording. Purchase invoices from subcontractors under DRC need the reverse charge applied in the VAT return. Train the office and check the accounting software's settings.
- **Get written confirmation** of each customer's DRC status (end user / not end user) at contract stage. Keep it on file.

## PAYE and RTI

- Submit a **Full Payment Submission (FPS)** on or before each payday.
- Submit an **Employer Payment Summary (EPS)** by the **19th** after the tax month to claim Employment Allowance, record CIS deductions suffered, statutory pay recovery, or declare no payment.
- **Pay PAYE, NIC and CIS by the 22nd** (electronic) after the end of the tax month.

**Year-end and benefits:**

| Deadline | What |
|---|---|
| 31 May | P60 to employees |
| 6 July | P11D and P11D(b) for benefits in kind |
| 22 July (electronic) | Class 1A NIC payment |

- Mandatory payrolling of benefits has been announced from April 2027 **(verify)**.
- Keep payroll records for at least 3 years after the end of the tax year (HMRC minimum). Longer is safer.

## Employer National Insurance (from 6 April 2025 — verify for the current year)

- The employer (secondary) Class 1 rate is **15%**, up from 13.8%.
- The secondary threshold is **£5,000 a year**, down from £9,100, so NIC now applies to much more of each salary.
- **Employment Allowance** rose to **£10,500**, and the rule restricting it to employers with a prior-year NIC bill under £100,000 was removed. Companies where the director is the only employee paid above the threshold still can't claim it.
- Class 1A on benefits and Class 1B follow the same 15% rate.
- **Impact:** a combined increase in employment cost per engineer. Factor it into labour rates and contract price reviews.

## Corporation Tax

| Taxable profits (no associated companies, 12-month period) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to £50,000 | 19% |
| £50,001 – £250,000 | 25% less marginal relief (effective marginal rate on profits in this band is 26.5%) |
| Over £250,000 | 25% |

**Marginal relief formula:** `MR = 3/200 × (Upper limit − Augmented profits) × (Taxable profits ÷ Augmented profits)`. Where there are no franked investment income distributions, taxable profits equal augmented profits.

**Worked example:** profits of £100,000 with no associated companies.
- 25% × £100,000 = £25,000.
- Marginal relief = 3/200 × (£250,000 − £100,000) = £2,250.
- Tax = **£22,750** (effective rate 22.75%).

**Associated companies:**
- Companies under common control, such as another company owned by the same director.
- Divide both the £50,000 and £250,000 limits by (1 + number of associates). Dormant companies are ignored, and certain passive holding companies can be ignored **(verify)**.
- Limits are also pro-rated for accounting periods shorter than 12 months.

**Deadlines:**
- Payment: 9 months + 1 day after period end.
- CT600: 12 months after period end.
- Large companies (profits over £1.5m, divided by associates) pay by **quarterly instalments**.

**Late CT600 penalties:** £100, then another £100 at 3 months, then 10% of the unpaid tax at 6 and 12 months. Higher fixed penalties apply for repeat lateness.

**Director extraction (discuss with the accountant each year):**
- salary vs dividends mix
- dividend tax rates (increases were announced for dividend income from April 2026 **(verify)**)
- director's loan account (overdrawn balances attract a section 455 charge if not repaid within 9 months of year end)
- pension contributions (employer contributions are generally deductible)

## Companies House

- **Accounts:** due **9 months** after the accounting reference date (private company).
- **Late filing penalties (private company):**
  - up to 1 month late: £150
  - 1–3 months: £375
  - 3–6 months: £750
  - over 6 months: £1,500
  - doubled if also late the previous year
- **Confirmation statement:** at least once every 12 months, filed within 14 days of the end of the review period. It confirms officers, shareholders, PSCs, SIC codes and registered email address.
- **Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 changes:**
  - **identity verification** for directors and people with significant control (compulsory from November 2025, with transitional timing for existing directors **(verify)**)
  - a registered email address
  - an appropriate registered office
  - planned software-only filing of accounts and changes to the filing options for small companies **(verify timing)**
- **Company size thresholds** increased for periods starting on or after 6 April 2025 **(verify)**:
  - small: turnover up to £15m, balance sheet up to £7.5m, up to 50 employees
  - micro: turnover up to £1m, balance sheet up to £500k, up to 10 employees
  These affect audit exemption and filing format.

## Pensions: auto-enrolment

- Enrol **eligible jobholders** automatically: aged 22 to State Pension age and earning over **£10,000** a year.
- Minimum contributions on qualifying earnings (£6,240–£50,270): **8% total, with at least 3% from the employer**.
- Submit the **declaration of compliance** to The Pensions Regulator within 5 months of the duties start date.
- **Re-enrol** opted-out staff every 3 years, and re-declare each time.
- Handle opt-outs and refunds within the statutory windows. Give new starters the correct information letters.
- Legislation allows the age limit to be lowered to 18 and the lower earnings limit to be removed in future. **Check whether this has commenced.**

## Capital allowances

- **Annual Investment Allowance:** 100% deduction on up to **£1m** a year of plant and machinery, including **vans**, tools, test equipment, IT and office equipment. It is shared between associated companies in some cases.
- **Full expensing:** 100% first-year allowance for companies on **new and unused** main-pool plant and machinery, with no cap. It is useful above the AIA limit. There is a balancing charge on disposal.
- **Cars are excluded from AIA and full expensing:**
  - new zero-emission cars: 100% FYA (check the current end date)
  - other cars: writing-down allowances at the main rate (CO2 up to 50 g/km) or special rate 6% (over 50 g/km)
- **Announced changes (verify):** the Autumn 2025 Budget announced a reduction in the main-rate writing-down allowance (from 18% to 14% from April 2026) and a new 40% first-year allowance for certain main-rate expenditure. Check the rates for the current year.
- **Double-cab pick-ups** are treated as **cars** (not vans) for capital allowances and benefit-in-kind from April 2025, with transitional rules for vehicles acquired before then **(verify)**.

## Vans vs cars: benefit in kind basics

| | Company van | Company car |
|---|---|---|
| Tax on private use | Flat **van benefit charge** (2025/26 about £4,020) plus a flat **van fuel benefit** (about £769) if private fuel is provided **(verify current year)** | Taxable benefit = list price × appropriate % (by CO2/electric range). Separate car fuel benefit if private fuel is provided |
| No benefit if… | Only **ordinary commuting** and **insignificant** private use (a written policy helps). **Zero-emission vans have a nil van benefit** | — (any private availability counts) |
| Electric vehicles | Nil van benefit for zero-emission vans | Low %: 3% (2025/26) rising 1 point a year to 5% in 2027/28, with further increases announced **(verify)** |
| Employer NIC | Class 1A at 15% on the benefit | Class 1A at 15% on the benefit |
| Capital allowances | AIA / full expensing (van = goods vehicle, design weight ≤ 3,500 kg) | WDA only (or 100% FYA for new zero-emission cars) |
| VAT | Input VAT on purchase normally recoverable | Input VAT normally **blocked** on cars with private use |

- **Mileage in own vehicles:** HMRC approved mileage rates are 45p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p. **Advisory fuel rates** (updated quarterly) apply to reimbursing fuel in company cars.
- **Tracking vans:** see the staff monitoring section in `operations/maintenance-contracts-and-scheduling.md`. Keep private use policies consistent with tax treatment.

## Management accounts: key metrics for a service business

| Metric | Formula | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Gross margin %** | (Revenue − direct costs) ÷ revenue. Direct costs are engineer labour, materials, subcontract and vehicles (if treated as direct) | Split by stream (maintenance, reactive, installation projects, monitoring). Installation projects usually carry more risk and variability |
| **Net profit margin / EBITDA** | Profit before tax (or EBITDA) ÷ revenue | Trend month on month and against budget |
| **Debtor days** | Trade debtors ÷ credit sales × 365 (or × days in period) | Rising debtor days drain cash. Compare with payment terms (e.g. 30 days) |
| **Creditor days** | Trade creditors ÷ credit purchases × 365 | Stretching suppliers too far risks account stops |
| **Cash runway** | Available cash (plus undrawn facilities) ÷ average monthly net cash outflow (or monthly fixed overheads) | Months the business survives without new receipts. Keep a 13-week cash-flow forecast |
| **WIP (work in progress)** | Value of work done but not yet invoiced (at cost or at recoverable value) | Unbilled completed jobs are a cash leak. Invoice promptly after completion or at each application date |
| **Deferred income** | Maintenance invoiced in advance but not yet earned | Recognise monthly over the contract term. Don't treat the whole annual invoice as this month's profit |
| **Recurring maintenance revenue %** | Contract maintenance + monitoring revenue ÷ total revenue | Higher means more resilient and valuable. Track it with contract retention |
| **Labour recovery** | Chargeable hours billed ÷ paid engineer hours | Links to utilisation (see the operations file) |
| **Retentions outstanding** | Retention sums held by main contractors | Diary release dates (practical completion and end of defects period) and chase them |
| **Aged debt** | Debt by age band (current, 30, 60, 90+ days) | Focus collection on large and old items |

**Construction contract payment rules (Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, as amended):**
- The definition of construction operations **includes fire protection and security/communications system installation**. It is wider than CIS.
- Rights include interim payments on longer contracts, payment notices, **pay less notices**, the right to **suspend** for non-payment (with notice) and **adjudication**.
- "Pay when paid" clauses are generally ineffective, except on upstream insolvency.
- The Act doesn't apply to contracts with residential occupiers for their own home.
- Diarise application dates and notice deadlines on every project.

## Credit control process

**Before the work:**
- Credit-check new commercial customers.
- Agree payment terms in writing (quote acceptance, T&Cs).
- Get a purchase order number where the customer uses them.
- Take deposits on large installations and staged payments on projects.

**Standard reminder cadence (example, 30-day terms):**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Invoice sent same day as work completed, with PO number, job sheet and certificate attached |
| 21 | Friendly pre-due reminder / statement |
| 30 (due) | Due date. Automated reminder |
| 37 | Chase by email and phone. Check for disputes early |
| 45 | Second chase. Escalate to a senior contact. Warn that statutory interest and compensation may be applied |
| 60 | Final demand / letter before action. **Account on stop** (no new non-emergency work; life-safety call-outs should still be considered carefully and documented) |
| 75+ | Debt recovery: Money Claim Online (county court), collection agency or solicitor. For companies, a statutory demand is possible for undisputed debts of £750 or more (take legal advice before threatening winding up) |

- **Pre-action protocol:** for debts owed by **individuals or sole traders**, the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims requires a specific Letter of Claim and 30 days to respond before issuing proceedings.

**Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 (business-to-business only):**
- **Statutory interest:** 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate. It runs from the day after payment was due, using the reference rate in force on 30 June or 31 December before the debt became due.
- **Fixed compensation per invoice:**
  - **£40** for debts under £1,000
  - **£70** for £1,000 to £9,999.99
  - **£100** for £10,000 or more
- Reasonable additional recovery costs can also be claimed.
- If no payment terms are agreed, payment is due 30 days after the later of the invoice or delivery. Agreed terms over 60 days must not be grossly unfair.
- The government announced a package of late payment reforms in 2025, including proposals on maximum payment terms, mandatory interest and construction retentions. **Check whether any have been enacted.**

**Good practice:**
- Invoice fast. The number-one cause of high debtor days is late or inaccurate invoicing.
- Resolve disputes quickly and pay undisputed parts.
- Keep evidence (signed job sheets, photos, certificates).
- Use direct debit for maintenance contracts where possible.
- Review aged debt weekly.
