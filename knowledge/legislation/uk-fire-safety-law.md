# UK fire safety law: Fire Safety Order, Fire Safety Act, 2022 Regulations, Building Safety Act and Approved Document B

Summary: A plain-English guide to fire safety law affecting a UK fire and security contractor and its customers. Covers the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, Fire Safety Act 2021, Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, Building Safety Act 2022, Approved Document B, responsible person duties, fire risk assessments (PAS 79), enforcement, and the Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland equivalents.

Status: Written September 2026 from general knowledge. This is not legal advice. Building safety law has changed repeatedly since 2021. Anything marked **(verify)** should be checked on legislation.gov.uk or GOV.UK, and serious matters should go to a solicitor or qualified fire safety consultant.

## Quick facts (most asked)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Main fire safety law for non-domestic premises (England and Wales) | Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (the "Fire Safety Order" or "FSO") |
| Who is responsible? | The **responsible person**: the employer (for a workplace), otherwise the person in control of the premises for a business, or the owner |
| Must a fire risk assessment be written down? | **Yes, in full, for all responsible persons in England and Wales since 1 October 2023** (Building Safety Act changes). Before that, only where there were 5+ employees, a licence or an alterations notice |
| How often is a fire risk assessment reviewed? | No fixed legal interval: "regularly" and whenever there is reason to suspect it is no longer valid or significant change occurs. Annual review is common good practice |
| Duty to maintain fire safety equipment | Yes. Facilities, equipment and devices must be kept in an efficient state, efficient working order and good repair (Article 17 of the FSO) |
| Who enforces? | Fire and rescue authorities (West Yorkshire Fire & Rescue Service locally) for most premises. The Building Safety Regulator for higher-risk residential buildings under the Building Safety Act regime. Local authorities for housing under the Housing Act 2004 |
| Maximum penalty | Unlimited fine and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment on indictment for serious offences |
| Fire Safety Act 2021 effect | Confirms the FSO covers the **structure, external walls (including cladding, balconies, windows) and flat entrance doors** of multi-occupied residential buildings. In force from 16 May 2022 |
| Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 | In force 23 January 2023. Duties for multi-occupied residential buildings: resident information, fire door checks (buildings over 11 m) and extra duties for high-rise (18 m+ / 7+ storeys) |
| Higher-risk building (Building Safety Act) | At least 18 m high **or** at least 7 storeys, with at least 2 residential units (during occupation) |

## Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005

**Scope:**
- Applies in England and Wales to almost all non-domestic premises.
- Also applies to the common parts of blocks of flats and HMOs.
- Doesn't apply to the inside of single private dwellings.

**Responsible person and duty holders:**
- The **responsible person** is the employer (for a workplace under their control), or otherwise the person in control of the premises in connection with a trade, business or undertaking, or the owner.
- There can be **more than one responsible person** (for example a landlord and tenants). They must cooperate and coordinate.
- **Duty holders** are other people with control of the premises to some extent, such as a managing agent or a **maintenance contractor under contract**. They have duties relating to matters within their control. **A fire alarm maintenance company can therefore be a duty holder for the maintenance it has contracted to do.** This is why contracts, service records and written defect reports matter.

**Key duties (paraphrased):**
- Carry out a suitable and sufficient **fire risk assessment**, keep it under review, and record it **(in full since October 2023)**.
- Apply the principles of prevention: avoid risks, evaluate risks, combat risks at source, and give collective measures priority.
- Make fire safety arrangements: planning, organisation, control, monitoring and review.
- Provide appropriate **fire detection and warning**, and fire-fighting equipment.
- Keep **emergency routes and exits** clear, signed and lit. Emergency lighting is required where needed.
- Have procedures for serious and imminent danger (evacuation plans, drills).
- **Maintain** fire safety facilities, equipment and devices (Article 17). This is the legal hook for servicing contracts.
- Appoint **competent persons** to assist.
- Give information and training to employees, and information to other employers and to residents where relevant.
- Take extra care with dangerous substances (see also DSEAR 2002).

**Changes from the Building Safety Act 2022 (section 156), in force 1 October 2023:**
- All responsible persons must **record the full fire risk assessment** and their fire safety arrangements.
- Record the name and organisation of anyone engaged to carry out or review the fire risk assessment.
- Stronger duties to cooperate and share information between responsible persons, and to hand over fire safety information to an incoming responsible person.
- Residents in buildings with 2+ domestic premises must be given relevant fire safety information.
- Increased penalties for some offences, including impersonating a fire inspector and obstructing an inspector.

## Fire Safety Act 2021

- It amends the FSO. It clarifies that in buildings with **two or more sets of domestic premises**, the FSO applies to:
  - the building's structure and external walls, including windows, balconies, cladding and attachments
  - **all doors between domestic premises and common parts** (flat entrance doors)
- Responsible persons must therefore assess and manage risks from external walls and flat entrance doors.
- The Home Office's risk-based prioritisation guidance helped responsible persons plan updates to their fire risk assessments **(historic, for context)**.

## Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 (in force 23 January 2023)

| Building | Duties on the responsible person |
|---|---|
| All multi-occupied residential buildings (2+ flats) | Give residents fire safety instructions (how to report a fire, the evacuation strategy) and information on the importance of fire doors |
| Over 11 m high | Plus: **quarterly checks of communal fire doors** (including self-closers), and **annual "best endeavours" checks of flat entrance doors** |
| High-rise (18 m+ or 7+ storeys) | Plus: building plans and floor plans to the FRS (electronically) and in a **secure information box**; external wall information to the FRS; **monthly checks of lifts for firefighters and of essential fire-fighting equipment** (faults not fixed within 24 hours must be reported to the FRS); **wayfinding signage** for firefighters |

**Sales point:** fire alarm, EVC, dry riser, smoke control and door checks in residential blocks are regulated activities. Contracted monthly checks and fast fault reporting are valuable services.

## Building Safety Act 2022 (BSA)

**For higher-risk buildings (HRBs) in England:**
- **Definition:** residential buildings at least 18 m or 7 storeys with 2+ residential units. During design and construction, the definition also covers care homes and hospitals meeting the height test.
- The **Building Safety Regulator (BSR)** is the building control authority for HRBs. It was set up within HSE. The government announced it will become a standalone body **(verify current status)**.
- **Gateways:**
  - Gateway 1 at planning (fire statement).
  - Gateway 2 before construction starts: the BSR must approve building control applications, and significant changes need approval.
  - Gateway 3 at completion: a completion certificate is needed before occupation.
- **In occupation:**
  - a Principal Accountable Person and Accountable Persons
  - registration of the building
  - a safety case report
  - the **golden thread** of building information
  - mandatory occurrence reporting
  - resident engagement

**Implications for fire and security contractors on HRB projects:**
- Major changes to fire systems can be "controlled changes" needing BSR approval. Programme delays are common, so allow for them.
- Provide accurate, digital as-built information (drawings, cause and effect, certificates, product data) for the golden thread.
- Expect clients to check contractor **competence** and third-party certification more rigorously.

**For all building work (England, from 1 October 2023):**
- Building Regulations dutyholder duties apply: the client, principal designer and principal contractor (building regulations roles, separate from CDM roles).
- Anyone carrying out design or building work must be **competent** for it and must plan, manage and monitor their work to comply with the Building Regulations.
- A subcontractor installing a fire alarm system as part of building work has these duties for its own work.

**Other BSA points:**
- Registration of building inspectors and building control approvers.
- A new construction products regime under development.
- Extended limitation periods for claims under the Defective Premises Act 1972. Retrospectively this is 30 years for past work, and 15 years for future claims **(verify)**. **Keep installation records for the long term.**

## Building Regulations and Approved Document B

- **Building Regulations 2010 (England), Part B (Fire safety):**
  - **B1:** means of warning and escape (this is where fire alarms and emergency lighting sit)
  - **B2:** internal fire spread (linings)
  - **B3:** internal fire spread (structure, compartmentation, fire-stopping)
  - **B4:** external fire spread
  - **B5:** access and facilities for the fire service
- **Approved Document B** gives statutory guidance:
  - **Volume 1:** dwellings. New dwellings need at least Grade D2 Category LD3 smoke/heat alarms to BS 5839-6.
  - **Volume 2:** buildings other than dwellings. It references BS 5839-1, BS 5266-1 and others.
  - Alternative routes such as BS 9999 (non-residential) and BS 9991 (residential) may be used.
- **Recent significant amendments (verify details and transitional dates):**
  - Sprinklers in new blocks of flats over 11 m.
  - Secure information boxes (over 11 m) and evacuation alert systems (18 m+) in new residential buildings.
  - A requirement for a **second staircase in new residential buildings over 18 m**, with a transitional period ending around 30 September 2026 **(verify)**.
  - Restrictions on combustible materials in external walls.
- **Regulation 38 (fire safety information):** on completion of building work (or a material change of use) in buildings covered by the FSO, the person doing the work must give fire safety information to the responsible person. That includes as-built fire systems information, operation and maintenance details, and certificates. **Contractors should supply full O&M and certificates at handover.**
- **Notification:** fire alarm work that forms part of a building project is dealt with through building control for that project. Standalone like-for-like replacement in existing non-domestic premises is not always notifiable. Check with building control where there is doubt **(verify)**. Electrical work in dwellings is covered by Part P (competent person schemes).

## Fire risk assessment (FRA) and PAS 79

- **PAS 79-1:2020** covers non-domestic premises. **PAS 79-2:2020** covers housing (blocks of flats and similar). Both set a structured methodology and a model report format.
- **Typical five-step approach:**
  1. identify fire hazards (ignition, fuel, oxygen)
  2. identify people at risk (including vulnerable people, lone workers, visitors)
  3. evaluate, remove or reduce the risks and protect people with fire precautions
  4. record findings, plan, inform, instruct and train
  5. review
- **Outputs:**
  - a risk rating
  - an action plan with priorities and timescales
  - the recommended fire alarm category (for example "L2/M")
  - emergency lighting provision
  - extinguisher provision
  - management actions such as weekly alarm tests, drills and logbook keeping
- **Competence:** the responsible person must use a competent assessor. Third-party certification (for example BAFE SP205 for companies) or registration with professional bodies is evidence of competence.
- **External walls:** PAS 9980:2022 covers fire risk appraisal of external wall construction in blocks of flats.
- **Opportunities for a fire and security contractor:**
  - FRA action plans often generate work: category upgrades, extra detectors, emergency lighting, door holders, signage, extinguishers.
  - Ask customers for the relevant FRA extract when quoting, and quote to meet it.
  - Don't carry out FRAs unless competent and insured for that work.

## Enforcement

- Enforcing authorities (fire and rescue authorities for most premises) carry out risk-based audits and investigations after fires or complaints.
- **Notices under the FSO:**
  - **Alterations notice:** the responsible person must notify the authority before making specified changes, for premises that are high risk or could become so.
  - **Enforcement notice:** requires specified breaches to be remedied within a set period, with a right of appeal to the magistrates' court.
  - **Prohibition notice:** prohibits or restricts use of the premises where the risk to people is so serious. It can take immediate effect.
- **Offences and penalties:**
  - failing to comply with duties where this puts people at risk of death or serious injury
  - failing to comply with notices
  - obstructing inspectors
  - Penalties are unlimited fines, and up to 2 years' imprisonment for serious offences on indictment.
- Many fire and rescue services publish enforcement registers.
- **Customer conversation point:** a customer under an enforcement notice often needs urgent, well-documented remedial work. Keep certificates and correspondence tidy, as they will be seen by the enforcing officer.

## Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland (one line each)

- **Scotland:** the Fire (Scotland) Act 2005 (Part 3) and the Fire Safety (Scotland) Regulations 2006 place duties on "duty holders", enforced by the Scottish Fire and Rescue Service. Building standards follow the Scottish Technical Handbooks, and interlinked alarm rules for all homes apply from February 2022.
- **Wales:** the Fire Safety Order applies (as amended by the Fire Safety Act 2021 and BSA changes where they extend to Wales). Welsh building regulations and the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016 alarm rules apply, and Wales is developing its own building safety legislation **(verify status)**.
- **Northern Ireland:** the Fire and Rescue Services (Northern Ireland) Order 2006 and the Fire Safety Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2010 apply, enforced by the Northern Ireland Fire & Rescue Service, with separate NI building regulations.
