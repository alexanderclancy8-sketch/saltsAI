# Certification, accreditation and competency: BAFE, NSI, SSAIB, FIA, cards, BS 7671 and site safety

Summary: A guide to the third-party certification schemes, training, site cards and safety competencies a UK fire and security company needs. Covers BAFE, NSI, SSAIB, FIA, ECS/CSCS, BS 7671, working at height and asbestos, plus what auditors look for and how long to keep records.

Status: Written September 2026 from general knowledge. Scheme rules, code numbers and card types change, so check anything marked **(verify)** with the scheme owner (BAFE, NSI, SSAIB, ECS, CSCS, IET) before relying on it.

## Quick facts (most asked)

| Scheme / item | What it is | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **BAFE SP203-1** | Third-party certification for fire detection and fire alarm system companies, in modules: design, installation, commissioning/handover, maintenance | Fire alarm companies wanting recognised competence. Many clients, insurers and FRAs expect it |
| **BAFE SP203-4** | Emergency lighting systems company scheme **(verify reference/scope)** | Emergency lighting contractors |
| **BAFE SP205** | Life safety fire risk assessment company scheme | Firms that carry out fire risk assessments |
| **BAFE SP101** | Portable fire extinguisher servicing company scheme, with an associated technician competence standard (ST104) **(verify)** | Extinguisher service providers |
| **NSI Gold** | NSI certification including ISO 9001 quality management plus technical codes of practice | Security (and fire) companies wanting the highest NSI tier |
| **NSI Silver** | NSI technical certification without the full ISO 9001 element **(verify scheme structure)** | Smaller security firms |
| **NSI NCP 109** | NSI code of practice for fire detection and alarm systems **(verify current version)** | NSI fire-certificated firms |
| **NSI NCP 104** | The brief references this code. **Confirm its exact title and scope (CCTV or access control) with NSI before quoting it** | NSI security-certificated firms |
| **SSAIB** | UKAS-accredited certification body for security systems (intruder, CCTV, access control), fire (including BAFE schemes), monitoring and guarding | An alternative to NSI. Recognised by police for URN systems |
| **FIA training** | Fire Industry Association courses and qualifications (foundation, design, installation, commissioning, maintenance, EL, suppression) | Engineers and designers |
| **ECS / CSCS cards** | Site skills cards proving qualifications and H&S awareness | Most main contractor sites demand one |
| **BS 7671** | IET Wiring Regulations (18th Edition, as amended) | Anyone doing electrical installation work, including mains supplies to panels |
| **BS 7858** | Security screening (vetting) of staff | Required by NSI/SSAIB for staff with access to security system information |

## Why third-party certification matters

- The Fire Safety Order requires the responsible person to use **competent** people. Government guidance points to **third-party certification** as a way to show competence.
- Insurers, FRAs, building control, housing providers and main contractors increasingly **require** BAFE, NSI or SSAIB certification in tenders.
- Police URN response for intruder and BS 8418 CCTV systems needs an **NSI- or SSAIB-certificated** installer.
- Certification gives a defensible audit trail if something goes wrong: a fire, a claim, an enforcement action.

## BAFE schemes (fire)

**SP203-1 (fire detection and alarm):**
- Modules are certificated separately: **design**, **installation**, **commissioning and handover**, **maintenance**. A company may hold one or all.
- The company issues a **BAFE Certificate of Compliance** for each relevant job/module, alongside the BS 5839-1 certificates.
- Scheme requirements include:
  - working to BS 5839-1 / BS 5839-6
  - a documented management system
  - competent staff (training and assessment records)
  - subcontractor control
  - calibrated equipment
  - customer complaints handling
  - annual surveillance audits, including site inspections
- It is delivered through UKAS-accredited, BAFE-licensed certification bodies. Examples include NSI, SSAIB and others. **Check BAFE's current list (verify).**
- The BAFE register lets customers verify certificated companies. Keep the listing accurate (scope and address).

**SP205 (fire risk assessment):**
- Company certification for life safety fire risk assessments. It requires competent assessors (experience, qualifications, CPD, professional body registration), technical review of reports and PAS 79-style methodology.

**SP101 (portable extinguishers):**
- Company certification for supply, commissioning and maintenance to BS 5306-3 and BS 5306-8. Technicians are assessed against the associated technician standard **(verify ST104 reference)**.

**Other BAFE schemes** exist, for example for emergency lighting and kitchen suppression. Check the BAFE website for current scheme numbers and scopes **(verify)**.

## NSI (National Security Inspectorate)

- A UKAS-accredited certification body for security and fire services.
- **NSI Gold:**
  - Certification to ISO 9001 plus the relevant technical standards and NSI codes of practice.
  - Stronger in tenders.
  - Requires a documented quality management system, internal audits and management review.
- **NSI Silver:**
  - Technical compliance with the standards and codes of practice.
  - Commonly the entry route for smaller firms **(verify the current distinction)**.
- **Fire:**
  - NSI Fire Gold/Silver schemes incorporate BAFE SP203-1 modules and NSI's fire code of practice **(NCP 109 — verify version)**.
- **Security:**
  - Intruder and hold-up alarms, CCTV, access control, ARCs, keyholding and guarding each have their own standards and NSI codes.
  - Code numbers (such as NCP 104) should be confirmed against NSI's current document list before quoting them to an auditor or client.
- **Recognition:**
  - NSI-certificated companies are recognised by police forces for URN applications (intruder and BS 8418 CCTV).

## SSAIB (Security Systems and Alarms Inspection Board)

- A UKAS-accredited certification body offering:
  - security systems certification (intruder, hold-up, CCTV, access control)
  - fire certification (including BAFE schemes)
  - monitoring centres, guarding and ISO 9001
- Recognised by the police for URN purposes, in the same way as NSI.
- Firms choose between NSI and SSAIB on cost, audit style, client requirements and scheme scope. **Both are acceptable to police and most insurers. Check a specific tender's wording.**

## Other certification and accreditation often requested

| Item | What it is |
|---|---|
| ISO 9001 | Quality management. Part of NSI Gold. Often asked for in public sector tenders |
| ISO 14001 / ISO 45001 | Environmental and health and safety management. Sometimes requested by larger clients |
| SSIP schemes (e.g. CHAS, SafeContractor, Constructionline, SMAS) | Health and safety pre-qualification recognised across Safety Schemes in Procurement members. Many main contractors and FM companies require one |
| **Cyber Essentials** / Cyber Essentials Plus | Government-backed cyber security certification. Increasingly asked for by clients with IP-connected fire and security systems, and required for some public sector contracts |
| LPCB (e.g. LPS 1014 for fire alarm firms) | Loss Prevention Certification Board company schemes. Some insurers prefer LPCB listing **(verify scheme numbers)** |
| Secured by Design | Police-backed initiative. Products and developments meeting SBD standards |
| Manufacturer accreditation | For example approved installer status for particular panel brands, needed for software, training and parts access |

## Training and qualifications

**FIA (Fire Industry Association):**
- Trade association that publishes codes of practice and guidance, and runs courses.
- Courses cover:
  - fire detection and alarm foundation, design, installation, commissioning and maintenance units
  - emergency lighting
  - gaseous and kitchen suppression
  - fire risk assessment
- There are also regulated qualifications linked to these units **(verify current awarding bodies)**.

**Apprenticeships:**
- The **Fire, Emergency and Security Systems Technician** apprenticeship (Level 3) covers fire, emergency lighting, security and CCTV/access control pathways.

**Other bodies and training:**
- **Manufacturer training** on panel programming and software. It is often a condition of access to engineer software, so keep certificates.
- **BSIA** (British Security Industry Association) is the security trade association. It publishes guidance.
- **FETA** (Fire Extinguishing Trades Association) covers the extinguisher and suppression trade and training.
- **ICEL** (Industry Committee for Emergency Lighting) covers emergency lighting guidance and registration schemes.
- **Electrical qualifications:**
  - 18th Edition (BS 7671) course: City & Guilds 2382 or equivalent.
  - Inspection and testing: 2391 or equivalent, for those doing mains work and testing.

**Keep a competency matrix** for each engineer:
- qualifications with expiry dates
- manufacturer training per product
- cards
- driving licence checks
- H&S training
- asbestos awareness refresh date
- IPAF/PASMA
- BS 7858 vetting status

## ECS and CSCS cards

- **CSCS** (Construction Skills Certification Scheme) is the umbrella site card scheme in UK construction. Most main contractors require a CSCS or CSCS-affiliated card to get on site.
- **ECS** (Electrotechnical Certification Scheme, run by the JIB) is the CSCS-affiliated scheme for electrotechnical trades.
- ECS issues cards for **fire, emergency and security systems** occupations, alongside electrical cards **(verify current card names and qualification routes)**.
- Cards usually need:
  - a recognised qualification (or apprentice status)
  - a health, safety and environmental assessment (the ECS H&S assessment or CITB HS&E test)
  - renewal every few years
- **Check expiry dates**, because an engineer turned away at the gate is a lost day.

## BS 7671 (IET Wiring Regulations, 18th Edition)

**Current version:** BS 7671:2018 as amended (A1:2020, A2:2022, A3:2024). **Check whether a newer edition has been published (verify).**

**Relevance to fire and security work:**
- **Mains supply to fire alarm panels, EL circuits, access control PSUs and CCTV:**
  - Use a dedicated or suitably protected circuit.
  - For fire alarms, use a double-pole isolator labelled and secured against unauthorised operation (BS 5839-1 guidance).
  - Plan RCD arrangements so nuisance tripping can't disable life safety systems.
- **Regulation 521.10.202:** wiring systems must be supported so they won't collapse prematurely in a fire. This applies to all installations since the 18th Edition, so use metal fixings rather than plastic clips alone.
- **Safety services (Chapter 56):** requirements for supplies to life safety systems.
- **Testing:** insulation resistance, continuity, earth fault loop impedance and RCD tests on mains circuits. Record them on an electrical installation certificate or minor works certificate.
- **Dwellings:** electrical work is notifiable under Part P, and is normally done through a competent person scheme (NICEIC, NAPIT, etc.).

**Company decision:** decide whether engineers make final mains connections themselves (they need suitable qualifications and a scheme or certification process) or whether an electrician provides the supply.

## Working at height

- **Work at Height Regulations 2005** set a hierarchy: **avoid** work at height where possible, **prevent** falls (collective protection first: towers, podiums, MEWPs), then **mitigate** consequences.
- **Ladders and stepladders:** acceptable for short-duration, low-risk tasks where a risk assessment justifies it. Use professional-grade equipment (BS EN 131) with pre-use checks and periodic recorded inspection.
- **Mobile towers:** need competent erection (PASMA training). **MEWPs:** need IPAF training (PAL card) and a rescue plan.
- **Typical fire and security tasks at height:** detector testing (use extendable test poles where possible, which reduces time at height), sounders, cameras, beam detectors and ASD pipework.
- Include work at height in RAMS for every job.

## Asbestos

- **Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012:**
  - Employers must give **asbestos awareness training** to workers liable to disturb asbestos. Electricians and alarm engineers drilling and cabling are a classic example.
  - Refresher training is recommended **annually** (HSE guidance).
- **Duty to manage:** the dutyholder for non-domestic premises must have an asbestos management survey and register.
- **Before drilling, chasing or lifting ceiling tiles in any building built before 2000:**
  1. Ask for and read the asbestos register or survey.
  2. Check the location is marked as surveyed and asbestos-free, or use a method that avoids the material.
  3. If suspect material is found, **stop work**, don't disturb it, keep people away and report to the client and your manager.
  4. Refurbishment work may need a **refurbishment and demolition survey** first.
- **Record the asbestos check on the job sheet or RAMS.** Auditors and main contractors look for it.

## Other safety competencies commonly needed

- **Electrical safety:** safe isolation procedure, lock-off kits and voltage indicators (Electricity at Work Regulations 1989).
- **COSHH:** test aerosols, battery acid, adhesives. Have safety data sheets available.
- **Manual handling:** batteries, panels, extinguishers.
- **Lone working:** a policy, a check-in procedure and devices where appropriate.
- **Driving for work:** licence checks, vehicle checks and mobile phone policy.
- **Hot works and permits to work:** follow the client's system. Remember fire alarm isolation must be permitted and recorded.
- **First aid** and **fire marshal** training for staff.

## What certification auditors typically look for

1. **Management system:**
   - policies, procedures and document control
   - current standards held (the correct editions of BS 5839-1, BS 5266-1, etc.)
   - internal audits, management review, corrective actions
2. **Contract review:** evidence that each job's requirements were understood before starting: specification, FRA category, variations, insurer requirements.
3. **Design records:**
   - design certificates, drawings and battery calculations
   - cause and effect, sound level predictions or checks
   - variations with justification and agreement
4. **Job files:**
   - certificates completed fully and signed by competent people
   - BAFE certificates of compliance
   - commissioning test records (insulation resistance, sound levels, cause and effect)
   - handover documentation and logbook entries
5. **Maintenance:**
   - visits within the required intervals (no overdue six-monthly fire alarm visits)
   - defects reported in writing to customers and tracked
   - false alarm reviews recorded
6. **Competence:** a training and competency matrix, manufacturer training, CPD and supervision of trainees.
7. **Vetting:** BS 7858 screening files for security staff (right to work, employment and education history checks, gaps explained).
8. **Subcontractors:** an approved subcontractor list, competence evidence, supervision and inspection of their work.
9. **Equipment:** calibration certificates for multimeters, insulation testers, sound level meters, lux meters and battery testers, with calibration status labels.
10. **Customer complaints and feedback:** a log, investigation and closure.
11. **Insurance:** employer's liability, public liability, and professional indemnity for design.
12. **Site inspection:** the auditor visits a sample of sites and checks the installed system matches the documentation.
13. **Marks and logos:** certification marks used correctly (only for certificated activities, correct logos).

## Record keeping and retention (guide)

| Record | Suggested retention | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Installation certificates, design documentation, as-fitted drawings | Life of the system, **plus a long-term archive** (15+ years) | Building Safety Act extended Defective Premises Act limitation periods, golden thread requirements, future claims **(take legal advice on policy)** |
| Maintenance/service certificates and job sheets | At least 6 years (longer is safer) | Contract claims (Limitation Act 1980: generally 6 years; 12 years for deeds), certification audits |
| Contracts and quotations | 6 years after the end of the contract (12 if executed as a deed) | Limitation periods |
| Financial and tax records | At least 6 years | HMRC requirements for companies (see `finance/uk-tax-and-accounting.md`) |
| Training and competency records | Duration of employment plus 6 years | Evidence of competence in claims |
| BS 7858 vetting files | Per BS 7858 and the certification body rules (commonly duration of employment plus a set period) **(verify)** | Audit |
| Personal data (staff and customers) | No longer than necessary. Set a written retention schedule | UK GDPR storage limitation |
| Accident records (RIDDOR) | At least 3 years | RIDDOR requirement |
